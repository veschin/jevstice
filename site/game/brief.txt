Build The Glasswing, a small playable stained-glass butterfly tile puzzle. Deliver index.html in the current working directory. It must open offline as a single self-contained HTML file, with no other inputs, external assets, libraries, or network requests. You have file tools; the finished artifact is what will be checked in a browser.

Art direction: design a striking jewel-tone Art Nouveau specimen, with sweeping organic wing contours, elegant dark leading separating luminous glass panes, and one slim butterfly body on the centerline. Make the four wing lobes abundant and unmistakably different from one another, and make each individual lobe asymmetric within its square. Use violet in the upper left, teal in the upper right, ruby in the lower left, and cobalt in the lower right. Give the teal lobe a single conspicuous golden eye spot or crescent, above and right of the upper-right tile's center in the solved image. No other tile has a golden eye or crescent. At the solved orientation the four lobes meet the central body so the silhouette reads as one butterfly, not four icons. The body never rotates. Use real drawn shapes (such as inline SVG), not an emoji or a label in place of artwork. Treat it like a backlit specimen window; keep typography and surrounding decoration out of the way of the glass itself.

Geometry: present a playable board labeled Board and a static solved image labeled Target side by side at desktop size, both at the same scale. Each picture is a square of four equal square quadrants in a 2 × 2 layout. At a 1280 × 960 viewport both pictures must fit on-screen. On narrow screens the two may stack; keep their scales equal. In the solved orientation a board quadrant must look identical to the corresponding target quadrant. The artwork in a playable tile rotates about that tile's center, without moving the tile or scaling it. The visible tile center must be clickable.

Frozen startup: only the upper-right teal tile starts at 270° clockwise (one quarter-turn counterclockwise) relative to the target; its gold feature therefore starts above and left of its tile center. The other three are already in the solved orientation. The board is not completed on load. All four board tiles can be clicked; each click turns just that tile artwork exactly 90° clockwise. Keep the target always fixed and unchanged. Thus the known solution is to click the upper-right tile once; an upper-left click on a fresh board does not solve it. Show a distinct visible completion message only when all four tiles match. Supply a visible Reset button that restores exactly the same startup state and clears completion. Give tiles clear accessible names and keyboard activation, and provide a short instruction on the page. Keep the board and target visible after completion for a direct comparison.
