Two fares to the seaside

Two adults and two children, aged 4 and 11, want to go from Millcross to Brinehaven and back. The coach guide looks cheaper until a later notice changes the fare for one child.

Fictional route, operators and prices. The model runs are real.

Model comparisonsAll trialsFare excerpts

What should the family buy?

Take Gull Rail on Saturday. If the family can go on Wednesday instead, Tern Coach costs less. Each return covers both journeys.

How the fares add up

Gull Rail charges £18 for each adult and £8 for the 11-year-old. The 4-year-old travels free on both days, so rail costs £44: £18 + £18 + £8 [R1].

On Saturday, Tern Coach charges £16 per adult and £7 per child, including the 4-year-old. The total is £46: £16 + £16 + £7 + £7 [C1] [C3]. The correction overrides the £39 example in the weekend guide [C2].

On Wednesday, the 4-year-old travels free on the coach again. The total drops to £39: £16 + £16 + £7 [C1] [C3]. Rail still costs £44, so the coach now saves £5.

What the models wrote

All 17 delivered reports picked the right ticket on both days. Two GPT-6 Luna reports cited the right fares but added them incorrectly, one in each condition. The middle score was 8/8 for all three models in both conditions.

For each model, we show one delivered report per condition, selected by the middle score. Repeat numbers can differ. How we selected and scored the reports.

In Luna repeat 1 with Jevstice, the model saved the incorrect Saturday total before acceptance returned “No ordinary result check was judged”. The event log records no later edit to the report. See the ledger for tool calls and token counts.

How we scored the reports

We recalculated the fares from the packet and checked each delivered report against these eight questions. The score does not use Jevstice verdicts or reward formatting.

  1. Does the rail breakdown cover both legs and add up to £44?
  2. Does the Saturday coach breakdown charge both children and add up to £46?
  3. Does the report recommend rail on Saturday and show the £2 saving?
  4. Does it cite the weekend guide [C2] and explain that the correction [C3] overrides it?
  5. Does it show why the Wednesday coach fare drops to £39?
  6. Does it recommend coach on Wednesday and show the £5 saving versus £44 by rail?
  7. Does [R1] support the report's rail fares and age rules?
  8. Does [C1] support the coach return prices and [C3] the age rule on both days?

We sorted the delivered reports by score, then repeat number, and selected the middle report in each condition (the lower middle if two were delivered). These results describe one fictional task with three planned repeats per model and condition. They do not establish how either condition performs on other research tasks.
